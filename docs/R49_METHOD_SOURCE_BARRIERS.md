## 3. Source-claim evidence, not a cumulative maturity score

A new 18-source *purposive* chart differentiates **P** normative propositions, **S** vendor sourcing, **C** accessed contract instrument, **O** dated oversight episode, **R** documented supplier action, and **X** independently supported causal contract-remedy outcome. Markings `Y/U/N` mean evidenced in the consulted source, not determined, or not supported *by that source*. They are **non-ordinal, nonadditive statement-support fields**, not counts of government prevalence or success rates. They may be correlated when reports share parties or contracts; even a signed clause is not proof that its audit power was exercised.

- Only one chart row contains a directly examined contract instrument (North Northamptonshire); the other contract studies often report procurement and signed projects without locally examining original signed contract files.
- The Victorian audit describes an audit-linked image-retention compliance correction, not a model performance improvement or proven contractual remedy.
- An NHS procurement evaluation describes contracts signed in three clinical networks, but does not establish that an enforceable contractual penalty or model-correction remedy was used.
- A 2026 Federation of American Scientists memo reports 215 contracts and 3,771 provisions from three states in an ongoing research program; this is **close existing prior work**, NOT an original record quietly added to the frozen 2,556. It blocks any first-in-field claims about studying AI contract clauses.
- **Original U.S. federal award vs. agency oversight:** USAspending identifies DHS Clearview AI facial-recognition enterprise licenses under contract ID `70CMSD21P00000127` (2021 onward). A GAO official follow-up reports that ICE/HSI implemented a June 2024 account-training validation process for its Clearview AI users. This is an agency control, not proof of a supplier remedy, and GAO does not certify that the precise USAspending instrument is the one governing that process. **Faception**, a company separately named in old reporting of AI facial-profiling contracts, is not Clearview AI; vendor-brand and algorithm-type anti-joins are mandatory. See `R49_US_FEDERAL_AI_CROSSRECORD_COMPARISON.md`.

A government may build its own procurement-monitoring AI (e.g. Brazil's Alice); research may use AI to analyze bids; and a public-sector department can purchase a broad IT platform with unverified AI module scope. None is automatically a government purchase of externally supplied AI. Exact public buyer, legal supplier, product/service AI component, executed instrument/version, oversight power actually exercised, supplier response and measured effect must be traced separately.

## 5. Gating questions before stronger statements

1. Is there a government *buyer*, rather than merely public deployment or government-supported R&D?
2. Is a **third-party AI product or service** identifiable, rather than ordinary procurement analytics or non-AI IT?
3. Is a procurement decision supported by an **original signed agreement or award**, not a vendor's marketing claims alone?
4. Are any post-award audit, access request, monitoring, correction, sanctions, acceptance and dates **recorded**? Can the object (model, platform, data retention, workflow) be isolated?
5. If a public-service benefit is claimed, are numerator, denominator, measurement period, assessor and a credible attribution strategy present? If not, report the outcome as attributed experience, not a measured contract-remedy effect.
