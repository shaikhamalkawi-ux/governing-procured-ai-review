**Status (22 November 2026):** Author Review / **Submission HOLD**. This note records the current manuscript's bounded public provenance. It does **not** announce a peer-reviewed paper, a completed scoping review, or independent validation of all source contents.

**Title:** *After Government Buys AI: What Can Public-Sector Auditors Verify? A Critical Review of Supplier Accountability and Outcome Evidence*.

**Article type:** critical narrative review for public-sector auditors and AI assurance teams. It asks which documentary observations support (1) actual government acquisition of an AI system or service, (2) authority and post-award oversight, (3) an identifiable supplier response, and (4) measured public-service outcomes and any justified attribution.

**Current manuscript checks:** 16-page main article, 9-page current supplement (25 combined pages); 93 distinct cited source identities (not 93 systematically included studies); five main analytical tables and nine supplement tables; four main figures and one supplementary figure. The curated references include empirical scholarship, legal analysis, official procurement notices, auditing reports, standards, and related sources.

**Six source-role classes, from the project's reference-identity audit:** 34 official/public-administrative records, 25 empirical/observed research items, 16 legal/doctrinal items, 9 standards/methods, 6 reviews/methodology, and 3 other scholarly/sector references. These classes describe the **chosen bibliography**; they are not a representative estimate of the worldwide literature.

The separate historical search/screening checkpoint (R53) had 800 provisional decisions among 920 records, with 120 pending. It did **not** produce final eligibility, independent screening agreement or a completed PRISMA-ScR study cohort. Historic repository R51 screening results use a **different bibliographic universe and stage**; do not equate or add those counts to this manuscript's 93 cited identities.
