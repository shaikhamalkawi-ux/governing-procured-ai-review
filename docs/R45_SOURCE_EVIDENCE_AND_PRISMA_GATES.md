# 99aI R45 — source role, evidence-stage and unfinished-PRISMA controls

**As-of 8 November 2026.** Source-locked 2,556 provisional bibliographic identities, **580 AI-assisted single-pass title/abstract readings**, 1,976 still awaiting individual reading. The R45 112 new readings are deliberately sampled (32 high ranked, 32 hash-selected middle, 32 hash-selected low, 16 supplier/vendor cues). They cannot estimate model recall, sensitivity or a systematic inclusion rate. Every provisional label remains **not a final inclusion/exclusion**.

Source eligibility depends on a **public authority buying an AI capability from a discernible external supplier**. The following must stay distinct: researcher uses ML to analyze government spending; an agency develops AI internally; an agency co-develops a prototype under a research grant; an agency procures AI in a signed vendor instrument; the agency actually exercises contractual audit rights; the vendor responds to monitoring; an operational outcome is observed; and a causal outcome is supported by identification. These are not interchangeable events.

## Source contrast, not causal claim

The FAccT 2025 paper [Johnson et al., "Legacy Procurement Practices Shape How U.S. Cities Govern AI"](https://doi.org/10.1145/3715275.3732049) uses qualitative interviews with 19 city staff from seven U.S. cities to describe real obstacles such as a vendor not sharing performance information and city staff creating their own checks on a purchased gunshot-detection AI. These are **participant reports**; the article does not supply publicly independently authenticated signed contract clauses or establish a formal contractual remedy caused an outcome. Publisher PDF: https://facctconference.org/static/docs/facct2025-206archivalpdfs/facct2025-final258-acmpaginated.pdf .

The 2026 [Frontiers article on the "contractual vacuum"](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/artificial-intelligence/articles/10.3389/frai.2026.1916534/full) makes a normative argument for auditable supplier duties, not evidence of signed/enforced rights. [SmartHS](https://mlanthology.org/aaai/2018/zheng2018aaai-smarths/) describes deployed public-sector AI but does not document government procurement terms. [Torres-Berru & López Batista (2021)](https://www.mdpi.com/2079-9292/10/22/2873) uses AI to study the procurement process, not public-sector procurement of AI. Publisher AI-training copyright boilerplate is not an AI-methodology statement.

The public 13-row source-claim register is **13 propositions from 7 source families**, not 13 independent studies. No vendor contract enforcement and no causal effect is independently verified in these entries. Seven exact title/abstract similarity groups covering 17 bibliographic IDs remain **HOLD**, not auto-deduplicated.
