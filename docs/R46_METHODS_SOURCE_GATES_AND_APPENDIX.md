## Concrete source and inference lessons

**Original signed-contract evidence versus synthesis:** Ramsay et al. (2025) examined procurement and early rollout of chest-diagnostic AI across NHS networks. Its methods include **51 interview participants**, **57 observed activities** and **166 analysed documents**; the publication states three in-depth network contracts were signed in March, May and September 2024. This supports actual contracting timelines but **not supplier-sanction or breach enforcement** without separate documentary proof. Publisher full text https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12675025/ . Frozen register identity `BIB02573`.

**Interview-reported process versus instrument:** Johnson et al. (2025) conducted **19 interviews in seven U.S. cities** and documented alternative routes through city AI procurement, including low-cost acquisitions that can bypass some review steps. The original FAccT conference PDF was visually checked on pages 1 and 7: https://facctconference.org/static/docs/facct2025-206archivalpdfs/facct2025-final258-acmpaginated.pdf . It does not present independently obtained vendor-penalty certificates for each city. Frozen ID `BIB00442`.

**Source-level buy/build distinction:** Wan and Sieber (2025) surveyed **28 Canadian municipal AI projects** and explicitly compared in-house and outsourced projects. Do not reclassify all municipal AI projects as externally purchased contracts. Original publisher abstract https://doi.org/10.1080/10630732.2025.2477993 . Frozen ID `BIB00404`.

**Do not confuse evidence genres:** Hamscher et al. (2026) estimated questionnaire-model relations on **104 surveys**, rather than reconstructing a dated supplier sanction (https://doi.org/10.1108/IJPSM-10-2025-0507; `BIB00192`). Singh et al. (2025) produced a 14-factor structural expert model (https://doi.org/10.1108/JOPP-07-2024-0078; `BIB00296`). Evans et al. (2025) presents NHS stakeholder workshop lessons about procurement frameworks (https://doi.org/10.3389/frhs.2025.1608087; `BIB02591`). Nagitta et al. (2022) studied practitioner experiences and proposed human-centred procurement provisions (https://doi.org/10.1016/j.procs.2022.01.308; `BIB00840`). Alshehhi et al. (2025) reports **15 UAE public AI procurement projects and 40 experts in the secondary catalogue abstract**; the original full Methods were not accessed at this checkpoint (https://doi.org/10.1007/s13198-024-02653-9; `BIB00381`). Keep this original-versus-secondary tier visible.

**Supplier responsibility in legal argument:** Crawford and Schultz (2019) advocate treating private AI suppliers as state actors in some public-law situations; a legal argument is not evidence a court already imposed a remedy in a particular public contract. Original journal https://columbialawreview.org/content/ai-systems-as-state-actors/ . Frozen ID `BIB01188`.

**Boilerplate guard:** Thirteen machine-parsed records contain AI words solely in publisher licensing notices, not necessarily the underlying research topic. This is an auditable false lexical cue; it is not a computed error rate.
