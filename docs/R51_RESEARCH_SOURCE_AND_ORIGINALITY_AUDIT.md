## 3. Substantive scientific finding: no shortcuts from acquisition to accountability

Comparison of independently published sources demonstrates why evidence objects cannot be collapsed:

- **Acquisition routes** — Hudig, Kallina and Singh (CHI 2026, original 22-page published PDF, 16 interviews) describe AI arriving through ordinary IT purchasing, framework agreements, supplier updates and pilots. This provides interview accounts, not a census of signed contracts or exercised contractual remedies. Original DOI: https://doi.org/10.1145/3772318.3791968.
- **Buyer-side monitoring** — Johnson et al. (FAccT 2025, original author HTML, 19 municipal employees across seven cities) document some city employees developing their own performance measures for a procured gunshot detection system after vendor evidence was unavailable. This is participant evidence of oversight work, not an original executed contract, vendor penalty or measured causal effect. https://arxiv.org/html/2411.04994.
- **Actual contract timing** — Ramsay et al. (eClinicalMedicine 2025, original full text at PMC) describe procurement, evaluation, signed agreements in three observed NHS networks in March/May/September 2024, and 51 interviews/57 observations/166 documents. These are author-reported signing dates and implementation governance activity. They do not establish a dated supplier cure or its causal effect. https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12675025/.
- **Supplier secrecy** — Curtin and Fia (European Law Open, original publisher full text) analyse AI developed and provided under contract for migration authorities and procurement confidentiality. The work establishes a legal and institutional challenge, not an individually verified contractual sanction. https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/european-law-open/article/cracking-ai-secrecy-beyond-eu-migration-law-and-governance/0BDF7E96162E239A31321A000DD486F4.
- **Pre-award vendor assessment** — Scott et al. (JAMIA Open 2024, PMC full text) report **21 functional plus six nonfunctional criteria** applied to AutoML vendor submissions within an Australian health service. Evidence of evaluation is not evidence of signed performance terms or enforcement. https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11165368/.
- **Exit and withdrawal** — Packer et al. (JACR 2026, publisher abstract/PubMed) survey staff after a radiology AI tool was withdrawn; five of 40 reporting staff responded. Withdrawal does not equal supplier breach, formal enforcement, or demonstrated patient benefit. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jacr.2026.06.004.
- **Acquisition direction** — Cerezo-Narváez et al. (Frontiers 2026, original full text) analyse an AI-enabled *public housing* design/procurement-preparation system. AI used in the public procurement workflow does not necessarily mean the government bought the AI system from an outside supplier. https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13657976/.
- **Survey perceptions** — Hamscher et al. (IJPSM 2026) study 104 questionnaires on digital sourcing governance and perceived supplier performance. A perceived outcome and PLS-SEM association do not prove a documented contractual cure. https://doi.org/10.1108/IJPSM-10-2025-0507.
- **Close prior art, not new primary data** — Cusenza's 66-page 2026 legal working manuscript examines original state-level public AI contracts and amendments, including ten case-table rows and litigation-linked selection. This is close precedent for contracts, postimplementation clauses and challenges. The exact PDF was examined privately and is not included in the public ZIP. https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=6812838.

These examples were intentionally selected for source depth, not sampled for population inference. Detailed source-specific evidence limits, publisher URLs and source-unit families are in `data/R51_17_OriginalSource_ClaimAudit_PUBLICSAFE.csv`.

## 4. Methodological controls and originality boundary

This work does **not** invent: (a) a first-ever AI procurement study; (b) first contract analysis; (c) a new general evidence maturity hierarchy; (d) a validated model; (e) an estimated frequency of oversight failures; or (f) causal evidence that contractual remedies improve public outcomes. The closer contributions of Cusenza (state contracts), Hudig et al. (informal acquisition channels), Johnson et al. (actual municipal buyer experience), Kuehnert et al. (limitations of vendor self-disclosure) and Ramsay et al. (NHS procurement/implementation) already establish substantial parts of the landscape.

The **defensible value-added research question** is claim-specific traceability across *source roles*, with nonordinal fields for: buyer and supplier legal identity, AI component and acquisition period; documented authority; dated postaward action; identified supplier response; measured endpoint; and whether a justified design supports attribution. A source that demonstrates only one of these deserves an accurate narrow finding. A procurement or monitoring clause is not proof of exercise; an interview recollection is not a signed contract; an observed system withdrawal is not a sanction; and a subsequent metric is not a causal effect of contractual oversight.

R51 explicitly holds five related-publication/family relations, including Italian study title variants, China facial recognition contract-related studies, NHS AIDF outputs, an author network across FAccT/CHI, and the two Cusenza SSRN metadata/version entries. They are *not* automatically merged or counted independently.
